What Does a Primary Care Doctor Do?

A primary care provider (PCP) looks after your overall health over time, not just the problem that brought you in today. That usually means:

  • Prevention: Physical exams, screenings, vaccines, and lifestyle guidance.
  • Ongoing conditions: Monitoring and treating diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, thyroid conditions, asthma, and allergies.
  • Medication management: Reviewing what you take, adjusting doses, and managing refills.
  • Coordination: Ordering labs and imaging and referring you to specialists when needed.
  • Everyday illness: Care for many common problems, so you don't start from scratch each time.

Having one provider who knows your history makes it easier to spot changes early and avoid repeated testing.

When to Seek Emergency Care

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room for chest pain or pressure, signs of a stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, or speech difficulty), severe shortness of breath, uncontrolled bleeding, fainting, or a blood pressure reading of 180/120 or higher with symptoms.

Urgent and primary care clinics are not a substitute for emergency care.

Managing Diabetes, Blood Pressure and Cholesterol

These three conditions often have no early symptoms, which is why routine monitoring matters.

Diabetes and Prediabetes

An A1C test reflects your average blood sugar over roughly the past two to three months. By American Diabetes Association criteria, 5.7% to 6.4% falls in the prediabetes range, and 6.5% or higher is in the diabetes range. A diabetes diagnosis generally requires confirmation with repeat testing unless clear diagnostic circumstances apply.

Treatment can include lifestyle changes, medication, and regular monitoring.

High Blood Pressure

The American Heart Association considers readings consistently at or above 130/80 mm Hg to be high blood pressure. Management often combines home or clinic monitoring, medication review, and changes in diet, activity, and salt intake. Treatment decisions depend on individual health factors.

High Cholesterol

A lipid panel shows your LDL, HDL, and triglycerides, which your provider uses to estimate heart risk and decide whether lifestyle changes or medication make sense.

What is the difference between primary care and urgent care?

Primary care covers routine checkups, prevention, and ongoing conditions with one team over time. Urgent care treats sudden, non-life-threatening problems. Sahara West provides both.

Can a primary care doctor manage diabetes and high blood pressure?

Yes. Primary care providers monitor both conditions, review and adjust medications, and coordinate labs and referrals.

Does a physical exam include lab work?

It depends on your age, history, and risk factors. Your provider recommends labs when they are clinically useful.

How often should I see a primary care provider?

Many healthy adults go once a year for preventive care, and people with chronic conditions are seen more often. Your provider will recommend a schedule for you.
